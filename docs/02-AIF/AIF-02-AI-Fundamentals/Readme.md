# AIF-02 — AI Fundamentals

## Purpose

AI Fundamentals establishes the conceptual foundations needed to understand artificial intelligence systems and reason about their engineering design.

This pillar explores what AI is, how its approaches have evolved, how symbolic and statistical approaches differ, and how search, reasoning, and knowledge representation support problem-solving. It also introduces common AI problem types and the high-level lifecycle of an AI system.

The objective is to develop a clear mental model of AI rather than memorize algorithms or study the history of the field in isolation.

## Learning Outcomes

After completing this pillar, you should be able to:

- Explain the meaning, objectives, capabilities, and limitations of artificial intelligence.
- Distinguish artificial intelligence, machine learning, and deep learning at a conceptual level.
- Compare symbolic and statistical AI approaches, including their strengths, limitations, and trade-offs.
- Explain how problem formulation, search, reasoning, and knowledge representation support AI problem-solving.
- Identify common AI problem types and explain how problem requirements influence approach selection.
- Describe the high-level lifecycle of an AI system, from problem definition through ongoing assessment.
- Recognize when a question belongs to a more specialized AI or engineering domain.

## Prerequisites

No specialized AI knowledge is required.

A general understanding of computing and problem-solving is helpful. Familiarity with the Engineering Foundations (ENG) domain can provide additional context, but this pillar does not require advanced mathematics or prior machine learning implementation experience.

Mathematical foundations are covered separately in AIF-01.

## Recommended Learning Path

Study the chapters in the following order.

### 1. AI Concepts and Evolution

**File:** [AI Concepts and Evolution](01-AI-Concepts-and-Evolution.md)

Establishes the meaning and objectives of AI, the relationship between AI, machine learning, and deep learning, and the major shifts in AI approaches.

It also develops a realistic understanding of AI capabilities and limitations.

### 2. Symbolic and Statistical AI

**File:** [Symbolic and Statistical AI](02-Symbolic-and-Statistical-AI.md)

Explores two broad approaches to intelligent behavior: explicitly represented knowledge and rules, and statistical approaches that learn from data.

The chapter focuses on how these approaches differ, when their characteristics matter, and why hybrid approaches may be useful.

### 3. Search, Reasoning and Knowledge Representation

**File:** [Search Reasoning and Knowledge Representation](03-Search-Reasoning-and-Knowledge-Representation.md)

Introduces the formulation of problems in terms of states, actions, goals, and constraints.

It explains the conceptual roles of search, reasoning, inference, and symbolic knowledge representation in solving problems.

### 4. AI Problem Types and System Lifecycle

**File:** [AI Problem Types and System Lifecycle](04-AI-Problem-Types-and-System-Lifecycle.md)

Explores how AI problems can be classified, how problem requirements influence approach selection, and why developing an AI system involves more than choosing a model or algorithm.

It introduces the high-level lifecycle from problem definition to ongoing assessment.

## How to Study This Pillar

Treat each chapter as an opportunity to develop engineering judgment, not simply acquire terminology.

For each chapter:

1. Understand the problem or limitation that motivates the concepts.
2. Build a clear mental model before memorizing formal terminology.
3. Study the examples and identify the assumptions behind them.
4. Compare alternative approaches and explain their trade-offs.
5. Answer the interview questions without relying on the chapter.
6. Explain the key ideas in your own words.
7. Identify which related topics require deeper study in another pillar.

When comparing AI approaches, focus on why one approach may be suitable for a particular problem rather than assuming that one approach is universally superior.

## Completion Guidance

Consider this pillar complete when you can confidently:

- Explain AI, machine learning, and deep learning without confusing their conceptual relationships.
- Compare symbolic and statistical approaches using concrete examples.
- Formulate a basic problem and explain the roles of search, reasoning, and knowledge representation.
- Classify an AI problem and justify an appropriate high-level approach.
- Outline the major stages of an AI system lifecycle and identify important limitations.
- Explain your reasoning clearly enough that another engineer can challenge your assumptions.

The goal is not to memorize every historical development or algorithm. It is to establish the conceptual foundation needed to study, design, and evaluate more specialized AI systems.
